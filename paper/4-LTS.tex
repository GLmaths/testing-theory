\section{Labelled transition system}\label{sec:lts}

The labelled semantics is given in three steps: an LTS for programs, which
runs without memory; an LTS for the memory; and their parallel composition,
in which a program and a memory interact. Throughout, $\lambda$ ranges over
all labels, $\tau$ included, and $\mu, \mu_1, \mu_2, \ldots$ over external
actions. A transition carries, like a reduction, the choice $r$ that selects
it.

\subsection{Programs}

\begin{definition}[LTS of \lqCCS programs]\label{def:lts-prog}
The \emph{LTS of \lqCCS programs} is the LTS with choices whose states are the
programs of \eqref{eq:programs}, whose external actions are
\[
\mu \ ::=\ c!v \mid c?v \mid \req{U} \mid \initp \mid \mrecv{m} ,
\]
and whose transition relation, written $P \step{\lambda}{r} P'$, is the
least relation closed under the rules of \cref{fig:prog}.
\qed
\end{definition}

Besides the outputs $c!v$
and the inputs $c?v$ on channels, a program has external actions for its
quantum prefixes: it \emph{sends} the request $\req{U}$ for a gate and
$\initp$ for an initialisation, and it \emph{receives} the outcome of a
measurement with $\mrecv{m}$. Two programs in parallel communicate on a
channel with the rules ComL and ComR.

\subsection{Memory}

\begin{definition}[LTS of the quantum memory]\label{def:lts-mem}
The \emph{LTS of the quantum memory} is the LTS whose states are the
memories $\rho \in \Dens(\Hil_\Sigma)$, whose external actions are
\[
\mu \ ::=\ \memop{\sop{E}} \mid \msend{m} ,
\]
for $\sop{E} : \mathsf{Op}(|\tilde q|)$ and
$\M : \mathsf{Meas}(|\tilde q|)$, and whose transition relation, written
$\rho \mto{\mu} \rho'$, is the least relation closed under the rules of
\cref{fig:mem}. It has no silent transition, and all its transitions carry
the empty choice $\epsilon$.
\qed
\end{definition}

\begin{figure}[ht]
\small
\begin{mathpar}
\inferrule*[left=Op]
  {\sop{E} : \mathsf{Op}(|\tilde q|)}
  {\rho \mto{\memop{\sop{E}}} {\sapp{\sop{E}}}}

\inferrule*[left=Meas]
  {\M : \mathsf{Meas}(|\tilde q|) \\ 0 \le m < |\M|}
  {\rho \mto{\msend{m}} {\br}}
\end{mathpar}
\caption{The LTS of the quantum memory.}
\label{fig:mem}
\end{figure}

The memory has two kinds of actions only: it applies a super-operator, with
$\memop{\sop{E}}$, and it sends the outcome of a measurement, with
$\msend{m}$. A gate and an initialisation are both served
by the first: we write $\sop{E}_\mu$ for the super-operator requested by
$\mu$, that is, $\sop{E}_{\req{U}} = \sop{E}_U$ and
$\sop{E}_{\initp} = \sop{E}_{\mathsf{init}}$.

\Cref{def:lts-mem} follows the four axioms by which Khatri, Lami and
Wilde~\cite[Section 3.1]{KLW26} summarise the mathematical description of
quantum systems. The first two axioms give the states of the LTS, and the
last two give its two rules.
\begin{description}
\item[Quantum systems.] A quantum system is associated with a Hilbert space,
  and its state is described by a density operator acting on this space. The
  states of the LTS are therefore density operators. We take the partial
  ones (\cref{def:density}), whose trace may be smaller than $1$: the trace
  of a memory is the probability of the outcomes of the measurements that
  led to it.
\item[Composite systems.] The system composed of two distinct systems is
  associated with the tensor product of their Hilbert spaces. A memory of
  $n$ qubits is thus an operator on
  $\Hil_\Sigma = (\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes n}$. This is why an action names the
  qubits $\tilde q$ it is about: the operation acts on the components
  $\tilde q$ of the tensor product, and as the identity on the others.
\item[Measurement.] A measurement is described by a family of operators
  indexed by its outcomes, and the probability of an outcome is given by
  the Born rule. For a measurement $\M = \{M_m\}_m$ on the qubits
  $\tilde q$ of a memory $\rho$, the outcome $m$ has probability
  $\tr(\br)$. This is the rule Meas: the memory has one transition for each
  outcome $m$, labelled $\msend{m}$, to the unnormalised post-measurement
  state $\br$ (\cref{def:meas}), whose trace is the probability given by the
  Born rule. The probability is thus neither a label nor a weight of the
  transition: it is kept in the state that is reached. The outcome is
  \emph{sent} by the memory, because it is the memory, and not the program
  that asked for the measurement, which determines it.
\item[Evolution.] The evolution of the state of a quantum system is
  described by a quantum channel, a linear, completely positive and
  trace-preserving map. These maps are exactly the trace-preserving
  super-operators of \cref{def:sop}. This is the rule Op: the memory has
  one transition for each $\sop{E} : \mathsf{Op}(|\tilde q|)$, labelled
  $\memop{\sop{E}}$, to the memory $\sapp{\sop{E}}$, which has the same
  trace as $\rho$. The memory offers every evolution allowed by the axiom;
  the programs of \lqCCS only request two kinds of them, the gates and the
  initialisation.
\end{description}

\subsection{The composition of a memory and a program}

The labelled semantics of \lqCCS is the parallel composition
(\cref{def:par}) of the LTS of the memory (\cref{def:lts-mem}) and the LTS
of programs (\cref{def:lts-prog}). Its states are the pairs
$\rho \lpar P$ of a memory and a program; the symbol $\lpar$ is the
composition of LTSs, while $\parallel$ remains the parallel composition of
the syntax. The interactions between the two are given by the following
duality predicate (\cref{def:sync}).

\begin{definition}[Duality between memory and programs]\label{def:sync-lq}
$\syncM$ is the least relation such that
\[
\memop{\sop{E}_U} \syncM \req{U}
\qquad\quad
\meminit \syncM \initp
\qquad\quad
\msend{m} \syncM \mrecv{m}.
\]
\qed
\end{definition}

The transitions of the memory carry the empty choice $\epsilon$
(\cref{def:lts-mem}), so that an interaction has the choice $\epsilon \cdot t = t$ of the program. Since the
memory has no silent transition, a silent transition of $\rho \lpar P$ is
derived either by the rule Right of \cref{def:par}, when the program moves
alone, or by the rule Sync, when the program and the memory interact.
By definition of $\syncM$, an interaction is either a request of the
program, for a gate (rule Op of \cref{fig:prog}) or an initialisation (rule
Init), served by the memory (rule Op of \cref{fig:mem}), or the outcome of a
measurement, sent by the memory (rule Meas of \cref{fig:mem}) and received
by the program (rule Meas of \cref{fig:prog}). A measurement has one transition for each outcome $m$, to the unnormalised
post-measurement state, and all of them carry the same choice $t$.

Unfolding the rules, a gate, an initialisation and a measurement give
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\rho \lpar \tagged{t}{U(\tilde q)}.P &\step{\tau}{t}& \upd{U} \lpar P\\[2pt]
\rho \lpar \tagged{t}{\initp}.P &\step{\tau}{t}& \inirho \lpar P\\[2pt]
\rho \lpar \tagged{t}{\M(\tilde q \rhd x)}.P &\step{\tau}{t}& \br \lpar P[m/x]
\end{array}
\]
for a gate $U$ on $|\tilde q|$ qubits, and for $\M : \mathsf{Meas}(|\tilde q|)$ and
$0 \le m < |\M|$. The composition also has external transitions, by the rules
Left and Right: the actions of the memory, and all the external actions of
the program.
