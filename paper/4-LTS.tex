\section{Labelled transition system}\label{sec:lts}

The labelled semantics is given in three steps: an LTS for programs, which
runs without memory; an LTS for the memory; and their parallel composition,
in which a program and a memory interact. Throughout, $\lambda$ ranges over
all labels, $\tau$ included, and $\mu, \mu_1, \mu_2, \ldots$ over external
actions. A transition carries, like a reduction, the choice $s$ that selects
it.

\subsection{Programs}

The LTS of programs is given in \cref{fig:prog}. Besides the outputs $c!v$
and the inputs $c?v$ on channels, a program has external actions for its
quantum prefixes: it \emph{sends} the request $\req{U}$ for a gate and
$\initp$ for an initialisation, and it \emph{receives} the outcome of a
measurement with $\mrecv{m}$. Two programs in parallel communicate on a
channel with the rules ComL and ComR.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\small
\begin{mathpar}
\inferrule*[left=Tau]
  { }
  {\tagged{t}{\tau}.P \step{\tau}{t} P}

\inferrule*[left=Op]
  {U : \mathsf{Op}(|\tilde q|)}
  {\tagged{t}{U(\tilde q)}.P \step{\req{U}}{t} P}

\inferrule*[left=Meas]
  {\M : \mathsf{Meas}(|\tilde q|) \\ m \in \mathbb{N}}
  {\tagged{t}{\M(\tilde q \rhd x)}.P \step{\mrecv{m}}{t} P[m/x]}

\inferrule*[left=Init]
  { }
  {\tagged{t}{\initp}.P \step{\initp}{t} P}

\inferrule*[left=Send]
  {e \Downarrow v}
  {\tagged{t}{c!e} \step{c!v}{t} \mathbf{0}}

\inferrule*[left=Recv]
  { }
  {\tagged{t}{c?x}.P \step{c?v}{t} P[v/x]}

\inferrule*[left=IteT]
  {e \Downarrow \mathit{tt} \\ P \step{\lambda}{s} P'}
  {\ite{e}{P}{Q} \step{\lambda}{s} P'}

\inferrule*[left=IteF]
  {e \Downarrow \mathit{ff} \\ Q \step{\lambda}{s} Q'}
  {\ite{e}{P}{Q} \step{\lambda}{s} Q'}

\inferrule*[left=SumL]
  {K_1 \step{\lambda}{s} P'}
  {K_1 + K_2 \step{\lambda}{s} P'}

\inferrule*[left=SumR]
  {K_2 \step{\lambda}{s} P'}
  {K_1 + K_2 \step{\lambda}{s} P'}

\inferrule*[left=ParL]
  {P \step{\lambda}{s} P'}
  {P \parallel Q \step{\lambda}{s} P' \parallel Q}

\inferrule*[left=ParR]
  {Q \step{\lambda}{s} Q'}
  {P \parallel Q \step{\lambda}{s} P \parallel Q'}

\inferrule*[left=ComL]
  {P \step{c!v}{t} P' \\ Q \step{c?v}{t'} Q'}
  {P \parallel Q \step{\tau}{\{t,t'\}} P' \parallel Q'}

\inferrule*[left=ComR]
  {P \step{c?v}{t} P' \\ Q \step{c!v}{t'} Q'}
  {P \parallel Q \step{\tau}{\{t,t'\}} P' \parallel Q'}

\inferrule*[left=Restr]
  {P \step{\lambda}{s} P' \\ \forall v.\ \lambda \neq c!v, c?v}
  {P \setminus c \step{\lambda}{s} P' \setminus c}

\inferrule*[left=Rec]
  { }
  {\tagged{t}{\rec{X}{P}} \step{\tau}{t} P[\tagged{t}{\rec{X}{P}}/X]}
\end{mathpar}

Quantum operations are requests to the memory: $\req{U}$ asks for $U$ on
$\tilde q$, $\mrecv{m}$ receives the outcome $m$ of $\M$ on $\tilde q$,
$\initp$ asks for the initialisation of $\tilde q$.}
\label{fig:prog}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Memory}

The memory has two kinds of actions only (\cref{fig:mem}): it applies a
super-operator, with $\memop{\sop{E}}$, and it sends the outcome of a
measurement, with $\msend{m}$. A gate and an initialisation are both served
by the first: we write $\sop{E}_\mu$ for the super-operator requested by
$\mu$, that is, $\sop{E}_{\req{U}} = \sop{E}_U$ and
$\sop{E}_{\initp} = \sop{E}_{\mathsf{init}}$.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\small
\begin{mathpar}
\inferrule*[left=Op]
  {\sop{E} : \mathsf{Sop}(|\tilde q|)}
  {\rho \mto{\memop{\sop{E}}} {\sapp{\sop{E}}}}

\inferrule*[left=Meas]
  {\M : \mathsf{Meas}(|\tilde q|) \\ 0 \le m < |\M|}
  {\rho \mto{\msend{m}} {\br}}
\end{mathpar}
\caption{Memory LTS, $\mu \in \{\memop{\sop{E}}, \msend{m}\}$. The memory has
two kinds of actions only: it applies a super-operator, or it sends the outcome
of a measurement.}
\label{fig:mem}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The composition of a memory and a program}

The labelled semantics of lqCCS is the parallel composition of the LTS of
the memory and the LTS of programs (\cref{def:par}). Its states are the pairs
$\rho \lpar P$ of a memory and a program; the symbol $\lpar$ is the
composition of LTSs, while $\parallel$ remains the parallel composition of
the syntax. The interactions between the two are given by the following
duality predicate (\cref{def:sync}).

\begin{definition}[Duality between memory and programs]\label{def:sync-lq}
$\syncM$ is the least relation such that
\[
\memop{\sop{E}_U} \syncM \req{U}
\qquad\quad
\memop{\sop{E}_{\mathsf{init}}} \syncM \initp
\qquad\quad
\msend{m} \syncM \mrecv{m}.
\]
\qed
\end{definition}

The transitions of the memory carry the empty choice $\epsilon$, so that an
interaction has the choice $\epsilon \cdot t = t$ of the program. Since the
memory has no silent transition, a silent transition of $\rho \lpar P$ is
derived either by the rule Right of \cref{def:par}, when the program moves
alone, or by the rule Sync, when the program and the memory interact.
By definition of $\syncM$, an interaction is either a request of the
program, for a gate (rule Op of \cref{fig:prog}) or an initialisation (rule
Init), served by the memory (rule Op of \cref{fig:mem}), or the outcome of a
measurement, sent by the memory (rule Meas of \cref{fig:mem}) and received
by the program (rule Meas of \cref{fig:prog}). A measurement moves to one
unnormalised branch for each outcome $m$, and all the branches carry the same
choice $t$.

Unfolding the rules, a gate, an initialisation and a measurement give
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\rho \lpar \tagged{t}{U(\tilde q)}.P &\step{\tau}{t}& \upd{U} \lpar P\\[2pt]
\rho \lpar \tagged{t}{\initp}.P &\step{\tau}{t}& \inirho \lpar P\\[2pt]
\rho \lpar \tagged{t}{\M(\tilde q \rhd x)}.P &\step{\tau}{t}& \br \lpar P[m/x]
\end{array}
\]
for $U : \mathsf{Op}(|\tilde q|)$, and for $\M : \mathsf{Meas}(|\tilde q|)$ and
$0 \le m < |\M|$. The composition also has external transitions, by the rules
Left and Right: the actions of the memory, and all the external actions of
the program. They play no role in the Harmony lemma, which is about the
silent transitions only.
